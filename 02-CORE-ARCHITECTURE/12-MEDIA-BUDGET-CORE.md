0. PURPOSE


This document reconciles Media/Budget P0 with Golden Lead, Business Economics, Measurement, Meta/Execution and Director boundaries.


1. ROLE OF THE CORE


Media / Budget Core holds the professional truth of how scarce paid-media resources should be allocated, paced, scaled, reduced, tested or preserved relative to business goal, marginal opportunity, economics, capacity, realization, uncertainty and alternative use.


It does not define Business Truth, Golden Truth, causal truth or Meta capability by itself.


2. ROOT DISTINCTIONS


Budget ≠ Spend.
Allocation ≠ Delivery.
Average Return ≠ Marginal Return.
Attributed ≠ Incremental.
Predicted Purchase Probability ≠ Incremental Response.
Customer/Opportunity Value ≠ Incremental Media Opportunity.
Potential Quality ≠ Business Realization ≠ Realized Outcome.
Campaign Economics ≠ Business Economics ≠ Sales Realization.
Platform Learning ≠ Business Learning.
Exposure Need ≠ fixed Sample Rule.
Preserve ≠ Neglect.


3. NEXT-SHEKEL PRINCIPLE — WORKING CORE CANDIDATE


A media decision asks:
What is the best justified next use of the marginal unit of media resource, given the business objective and current evidence?


Candidate action classes:
ADD / HOLD / REDUCE / REALLOCATE / RESERVE / TEST / WAIT / PRESERVE.


The exact “Next-Shekel” name/object is not yet canonical.


4. DECISION INPUTS


A material budget decision may need:
- local business goal / Desired Work;
- campaign/strategy role;
- Golden Lead/value implications;
- incremental media opportunity evidence;
- current and marginal cost/response;
- contribution/cash/payback when relevant;
- capacity/serviceability/opportunity cost;
- Sales realization;
- outcome maturity;
- uncertainty/causal evidence class;
- platform feasibility/delivery state;
- alternative uses;
- risk/reversibility;
- user/owner authority.


No fixed field list is required for every decision.


5. AVERAGE PERFORMANCE DOES NOT ANSWER MARGINAL DECISION


Average CPL/CPA/ROAS/ROI may describe historical performance but does not by itself answer whether the next shekel should be spent in the same place.


Scale decisions require a marginal-opportunity question and awareness of saturation/threshold/wearout/auction/market alternatives.


No universal response curve or concavity shape is promoted.


6. CUSTOMER VALUE ≠ MEDIA OPPORTUNITY


A highly valuable customer/opportunity may already have high baseline propensity or may require access/brand presence rather than a particular paid treatment.


A lower-value segment may exhibit higher incremental response to a given intervention.


Media must not convert Golden Value into a direct spend multiplier without treatment/economic evidence.


7. CAPACITY-ADJUSTED SPEND


More demand can destroy value when Sales/Fulfillment capacity is constrained.


Media decisions should consider:
- scarce capacity;
- displacement/opportunity cost;
- serviceability;
- handling throughput;
- desired work mix;
- business stability.


No universal capacity utilization target/cutoff.


8. ALLOCATION BY ROLE / PORTFOLIO


Budget can serve different roles: acquisition, access/discovery, retargeting/reactivation, experimentation, learning, protection/presence or other justified roles.


Role taxonomy remains Working/open.


Allocation should not be based solely on platform object/KPI rank.


Different roles may require different outcomes, horizons and evidence.


9. EXPLORATION / EXPLOITATION / LEARNING BUDGET


Some spend can be justified by information value when uncertainty materially affects future decisions.


Learning spend should have:
- explicit question;
- feasible discriminating evidence;
- expected decision value;
- acceptable business/media cost;
- stopping/reopen logic.


No fixed exploration ratio or learning-budget percentage.


10. EXPERIMENT SUFFICIENCY


Media does not own universal sample/exposure thresholds.


Measurement determines power/precision/design sufficiency relative to estimand, variability and decision/error cost.


Platform delivery constraints must be considered, but platform learning guidance is not a universal scientific sample rule.


11. PACING / BUDGET PROTECTION


Pacing should compare intended horizon/business window with actual delivery and material constraints.


Budget protection may include avoiding:
- accidental overdelivery/underspend relative to authorized plan;
- concentration that destroys learning or business capacity;
- uncontrolled scale before outcomes mature;
- reactive oscillation to noisy short-term metrics.


Exact pacing rules are local/platform dependent.


12. SCALE / DE-SCALE


SCALE candidate:
stronger supported marginal opportunity + business value + capacity + feasible execution + acceptable uncertainty/risk.


DE-SCALE candidate:
weakening marginal opportunity, capacity/serviceability pressure, realized economics deterioration, business-goal conflict or risk — not CPL alone.


Scale percentage is never universal.


13. REALLOCATION


Reallocation compares marginal alternatives, not just historical averages.


A transfer should preserve:
- roles and goals;
- expected marginal value;
- measurement/uncertainty;
- capacity/economics;
- platform feasibility;
- transition/learning risk.


14. RESERVE — WORKING ONLY


Reserve may have option value when future high-value opportunities/windows are uncertain, but external support is insufficient for a universal advertiser-side reserve doctrine.


Reserve remains Working Candidate / Needs Evidence.
No fixed reserve percentage.


15. PRESERVE / DO NOT TOUCH — CONTEXTUAL


Preserve is a legitimate contextual decision when:
- current role contributes to goal;
- evidence is current;
- no better marginal alternative is supported;
- change risks delivery/learning/causal interpretation or scarce opportunity;
- capacity remains balanced.


Preserve is not a universal “stability is better” doctrine and requires reopen conditions.


16. BUSINESS ECONOMICS BOUNDARY


Business Economics supplies:
- contribution definition;
- relevant costs/cost-to-serve;
- cash/payback constraints;
- capacity/opportunity cost;
- desired work/strategic value;
- local economic confidence.


Media does not invent margin/CPA/ROAS target from external benchmark.


17. SALES REALIZATION BOUNDARY


Sales/Lead Handling supplies:
- response/contact/qualification/opportunity/won-lost;
- throughput/capacity;
- treatment/process changes;
- outcome maturity;
- CRM reliability;
- realization leakage.


Low realization does not automatically prove weak acquisition/media opportunity.


18. MEASUREMENT BOUNDARY


Measurement owns:
- estimand;
- attribution/incrementality distinction;
- experiment design;
- power/precision;
- outcome maturity;
- response-curve uncertainty;
- contamination/multiple-change risks.


Media consumes this evidence for resource decisions; it does not redefine causal confidence.


19. META / TECHNICAL BOUNDARY


Meta/Technical supplies current:
- account/campaign budget architecture;
- bid/performance-goal controls;
- delivery/learning state;
- supported changes;
- signal eligibility;
- experiment/lift capabilities;
- edit/permission/readback constraints.


Media professional decision precedes technical change.
A Meta recommendation is not a budget/business rule.
Live feasibility requires Fresh Preflight.


20. DIRECTOR / ADVISOR / AUTHORITY


Director frames the current decision and alternatives.
Media provides professional resource-allocation judgment.
Advisor explains/recommends to user.


Material budget changes require appropriate explicit authority; professional recommendation does not create spend authorization.


21. GOLDEN LEAD BOUNDARY


Golden Lead defines multidimensional business desirability/value/fit.
Media must keep it distinct from:
- baseline propensity;
- incremental marketing opportunity;
- access/retrievability;
- realization capability;
- realized outcome.


No global Golden spend score/weight.


22. USER / BUSINESS ACTIONABILITY


Media may need user/business input/actions such as:
- confirm goal/priority;
- confirm budget envelope/authority;
- provide economics/capacity/desired mix;
- resolve operational capacity constraints;
- approve material reallocation/scale/test.


Ask only when decision-relevant and unavailable elsewhere.


23. TRANSFERABILITY


Structural distinctions can transfer.
Mechanisms are scoped priors.
Local response curves, elasticities, thresholds, weights, capacity numbers and economics do not auto-transfer.


User-facing communication should distinguish measured here / learned elsewhere / hypothesis here.


24. FRESHNESS / REOPEN


Reopen after:
- business goal/capacity/economics changes;
- creative/audience/auction/seasonality/market/route drift;
- Sales process/CRM change;
- new causal evidence;
- Meta platform/control change;
- material outcome maturation;
- contradiction/failed replication.


25. DO-NOT-PROMOTE


Do not promote:
- fixed CPL/CAC/ROAS targets;
- fixed exploration/learning/reserve ratios;
- fixed N/days/conversions/spend sample thresholds;
- fixed scaling percentage;
- transferable response curve;
- average KPI-only budget rule;
- attributed ROAS as causal incremental value;
- historical customer value as direct spend rule;
- global Golden spend multiplier;
- platform recommendation as business hard rule.
